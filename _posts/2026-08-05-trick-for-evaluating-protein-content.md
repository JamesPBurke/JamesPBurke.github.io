In a recent conversation with my sister, she mentioned that one guideline that dietitians and nutritionists sometimes use to evaluate a food's appropriateness for fitting into a high-protein diet is to look at the label, take the number of grams of protein in one serving of the food, multiply it by ten, and compare it to the calories per serving. If the number is lower, it is less appropriate. If the number is higher than the calories, that's a good sign[^1]. 

In the moment, I did some incorrect mental calculations which confused me as I tried to figure out why the one number (protein grams) should be in a 1:10 ratio with total calories. This blog post is my effort to do better math and figure out where that ratio actually comes from. Or how to make sense of it.

[^1]: Why would you eat a high-protein diet? It is one way to avoid losing muscle when you happen to be eating a calorie deficit. Like when your are trying to lose weight, for example. Or maintain a healthy weight.
