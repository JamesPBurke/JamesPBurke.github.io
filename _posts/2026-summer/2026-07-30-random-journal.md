# Diet

I've been eating fewer calories, and I returned to what's worked for me before: calorie counting and macro tracking. This has felt like the least onerous diet in my personal history of dieting and the secret is: figuring out a healthy combination of macros for my age, getting a decent idea of how many calories I need to eat in a day, and then (as it turns out) hitting my protein goal and calorie goal at about the same time.

I'm eating more protein because, at my age, muscle building and maintenance is really important to weight control and general health. So, going into calorie deficit means being careful that your body doesn't consume your muscles looking for energy. It's not keto or whatever. It's just making sure I don't starve myself of an essential nutrient.

I'm on summer break, and that means I can spend time thinking about my eating. My goal is to do as much up-front thinking that I can think less about eating during the school year, when it gets frantic.

If you're interested, I'm using [Cronometer](https://cronometer.com/) to track. But I've used MyFitnessPal in the past, and I'm sure there are plenty of other tools that would help.

# "Balanced Diet" as a Video Game

I've heard "a balanced breakfast" and "a balanced diet" millions of times without thinking about it much. But today, it hit me, that keeping things in balance is a kind of game. And with a calorie counter, it has a visual output.

{% include figure popup=true image_path="https://ik.imagekit.io/scurryday/postimg/20260730_bars.png"  alt="A nutritional tracking dashboard displaying daily macro targets and remaining balances, including Energy (1,034 / 2,199 kcal), Protein (79.2 / 176.0 g), Net Carbs (85.6 / 219.9 g), and Fat (34.1 / 68.4 g), visualized with color-coded progress bars." caption="The way Chronometer displays my macros and energy" %}

But now, after watching my macro intake as I eat all day, I *picture* a balanced diet as bars, like the ones here. This is me, a little more than halfway through today. Each of these macros is a different thing, but they are closely linked. Protein, Net Carbs, and Fat all supply the body with Energy (measured in kcal - kilocalories). Not only are these macros different things, I have to eat different amounts of them. Each is a different percentage of my overall diet. So, the challenge of a *balanced diet* is like a video game where you try to keep ***all*** the bars at around the same length by the end of the day. Or (ideally?) after every meal (balanced meals). ***OR*** after every bite (OK, that's silly).

In truth, I'm trying to get the Protein bar to the end right before or right as the Energy bar fills up. That means I've given my body the protein it needs at around the time I've reached my target calories. I'm not *too* worried if I go over or under in fat or carbs.

I guess hard mode would be to try to get everything even all day long, every day. Unnecassary, but it's a funny idea for a biological video game.

# A Bad Coffee Idea or The Best Coffee Idea?

I've been supplementing my diet experimenting with coffee concentrate and [Kachava](https://www.kachava.com/),[^1] which is a balanced meal-replacer powder. Vanilla-flavored. In my shaker, I mix 6 ounces of my coffee concentrate[^2] with one scoop of the Kachava powder (substitute any protein shake or meal-replacer shake, I suppose) and add 2 ounces or water, as the package specified that an actual serving is 2 scoops for 16 ounces of water. Then I add a little scoop of ice from my icemaker. About a handful, but it varies.

It's good. I suppose caffeine is also an appetite suppressant.

# The Odyssey and Indoctrination

Maybe you are or maybe you aren't seeing a bunch of online content about Chris Nolan's new film *The Odyssey*.

I haven't seen it; I do like Nolan's movies, generally; I do plan to see this one sometime. I read The Odyssey in high school, somewhat reluctantly. But I loved the 1954 film about the subject matter: [Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_(1954_film)). I was fascinated by the special effects when I was a boy. I don't think I paid a ton of attention to the story.

I'm vaguely annoyed that there seem to be a million angry voices telling anyone who will isten that this movie will bomb, it's somehow woke, it mangles "history", and it mangles literature. But it feels so engineered that it *almost* seems like an engineered PR campaign called for by the production company itself. I dunno. Who does?

But how about this. Are all the people who attack this film dilligent students who read The Odyssey in high school, paid close attention, and became enamored with the material--so enamored that they could be so insulted by this or that interpretation of the literature that it actually motivates them to complain about it? I'm going to say: no. I'm sure there *are* people like that, but I'm going to make a contrast here, since this overlaps with my business, which is education.

If we teachers could get students *this* emotionally invested in curriculum, we'd be doing it constantly, and we'd be doing it across disciplines. Teachers can and do make a difference, and people know this, which is why some bad actors accuse teachers of indoctrination.

But here's the thing. *This* reaction is what indoctrination looks like. Teaching someone to be kind and accepting of others--that's not what indoctrination looks like. Teaching people ideas that they may or may not agree with, and hoping they think about it one way or another--that's not what indoctrination looks like. Teaching someone to be so angry about something that they literally start posting it everywhere, unsolicited--that's what indoctrination looks like, and it takes generating anger.

In classrooms, we're not generating anger. All the time we want students to be regulating their emotions, and often we take steps to understand how to make that happen. So that students learn about themselves and grow. Even if we could get them to learn some math by getting them angry, there are many reasons it wouldn't be effective *education*.[^3] But someone is out there doing something different that requires making people angry.

See? Think of that when you hear about teacher/school indoctrination.

# Re: Obsession

*Obsession* is a terrific horror movie with some uncomfortable things to show you and to say. It's been widely celebrated, and I saw it this week on Peacock TV.

This movie grabs you for some unexpected and pleasingly unconventional *types of* scares thanks to the whiplash perfomance by [Inde Navarrette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inde_Navarrette) as Nikki. She's truly wild to watch. Fans of [Superman and Lois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_%26_Lois) knew her as Sarah Cortez, a completely uninspiring character that variably gained the ire of fans in the way female characters often do when they assert themselves.

I'm not an actor, so I don't know what it takes to play emotions that originate supernaturally, so she must have had some great direction and some iron-clad grasp in her mind of what incomprehensible thing was happening to her character. I'll let others go on with her well-deserved horror acting accolades.

The short synopsis is that this is a film about a weak-willed young man named Barron (or Bear, played by [Michael Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Johnston_(actor))) who can't get up the courage to share with his good friend that he has romantic feelings for her. In his frustration, he turns to the tropey One Wish Willow to bend reality, despite ominous warnings. The predictable ensues, but your expectations do not get in the way of the deeply unsettling story that unfolds.

My assumption going in was that we would get some sort of extreme horror version of *Fatal Attraction*. I was wrong about that, thank goodness. The title is extremely misleading, in that we are likely to assume that One Wish Willow produces a sort of obsession that gets out of control. But the truth is more sinister, gut-wrenching, and I'm sure wrapped up in some interpersonal gender dynamics that I'm too casual to completely expound on.

Johnson's performance as Bear is key to keeping us the roller coaster ride. He convinces us so completely of his absence of character that I'm absolutely sure you'll predict almost every move of his. Not every character has to surprise you; sometimes they just have to convince you that what you expect *should* happen this way, keeping you inside the narrative. Johnson will frustrate the living hell out of you putting Bear's courage seemingly *just* out of his reach, like an itch you never get to scratch. But you get the message of who he is, and he's consistent.

Obsession is worth a watch for horror fans.
