Lately I've been counting calories again, which is a real pain starting out, but it gets easier as you work out the macros on meals and snacks you routinely eat. And, for me, the behavior change comes as I'm trying to hit my targets.

I use an app to track what I eat, so I get a view of my macros and other nutrients. If you're looking for a recommendation, I've liked both MyFitnessPal and Cronometer. Currently I'm using Cronometer. But MyFitnessPal worked fine for free for me over many years.

I'm in the process of working out three recipes[^1]. They all will involve cottage cheese, an ingredient/food i have steered clear of for over half a century. But now I'm embracing it for convenience, low calories, and high protein. At my age I'm trying to lose weight carefully while not losing muscle, and that means making sure I'm watching protein intake. It's not that I nead protein like a weightlifter; it's that I'm worried my body will eat itself looking for calories.

Anyhow, my three recipes are for everyday breakfast, sustainable repeat lunch at home or at work, and a slightly more involved lunch that is more like an actual recipe. I'll give you the first two and just mention what the third is, as it's still heavily under development.

## 1: Buffalo Chicken Salad

This recipe is inspired by a relative's creamy cream cheese buffalo chicken dip.

### Ingredients:

* 100 grams of pressure-cooked chicken thigh meat (prepared beforehand)
* 113 grams (one serving) fat free small curd cottage cheese
* 20 blurps/squirts of Frank's Hot Sauce or equivalent
* 1 teaspoon Balsamic vinegar
* 1-2 teaspoons of your favorite salt-free all-purpose spice (like Penzey's or a rotissery chicken spice)
* Optional: a handful of grape tomatoes

### Instructions

Mix cottage cheese with hot sauce and spices, then fold in the shredded chicken. Once combined, add the grape tomatoes if desired.

[^1]: I use the term loosely, but it's close enough. Many of my diet meals are basically just combining ingredients.
