# Memories

Old social media photos from our 2016 trip to Europe cropped up in FB Memories, and I was shocked when I saw a photo Maggie took of me on the balcony. Ten years ago, and I was just about in the best shape I'd ever been in. Better than when I ran my first half marathon. Better than when I was lighter, and younger, because I had more cardiovascular endurance and strength.

Then I saw a photo of the year I was hired to teach. Still in OK shape. I'd lost weight over the summer before I started -- the summer of 2019. I was trying low carb diet that was ultimately not for me, but worth the experience while I was teaching a graduate course (Technology for Math Teachers) at UMass Dartmouth. I had gained a little bit during my Post Doc.  Injuries led me to ruin less, and I had learned to eat like a person who ran 30 miles a week.

Something mysterious happened between getting hired and June of 2026.

# Weight, Weight, Don't Tell Me

Weight is not necessarily an indicator of health. Let me get that out there.

But it's also not ***not*** an indicator, if you understand me. And if you understand it. It's highly personal. Not being a physician, I'll just say this: weight can be a visible puzzle piece in a person's health picture[^health-puzzle]. In my case, I'm a stress-eater. For valid, personal, medical reasons, my doctor has repeatedly suggested I lose weight over the last two years. That's not a wild request. It turns out that I'm gaining more than 5lb every year that I've been teaching high school. COnsider that indicator a puzzle piece, but with its own possible consequences that are **not fun**



[^health-puzzle]: If you suddenly lose a lot of weight, your doctor might ask you what's changed in your behavior, because that is a change, and he wants to know whether what he's seeing is unexplained. Same thing for weight gain. It raises questions for the person concerned with your health. Casually, people may ask you questions as well, but that is not a professional interaction. And there are secondary effects of weight gain or loss. There are disorders that become more likely if you go too far in one direction or another. And there are psychological conditions worth being concerned about when a drastic change happens. Weight is not health, but there are many examples of it as a puzzle piece as well as being able to change your health outlook.
