I am not sure I've ever used the word "nuptials" before, and I suspect I have not, because I _really_ thought it was spelled "NUPTUALS."

My daughter got married today. Well, it's yesterday now. A small crowd of her and her husband's close family were there, in a field, in a state park, under a gazebo and a tent. She has found a wonderful partner from a family who are a very nice bunch of people. It was a wonderful time, and they planned every detail of the event, which came off without a _serious_ hitch. 

We'd worried about the weather. We worry in my family. The forecasts were not great in the run up to the event. To be honest, the _long term_ forecast looked _quite_ good two weeks out. But I learned that there are often pretty notable differences between the long-range projections depending on your data source. And as the day approached, the projections began to become more ominous. 

The day before, the principal characters in this ceremony met on Zoom to nail down the details. At that point, you could choose which forecast you preferred: the ones that said rain and thunder, the ones that said the day would be clear, and the ones that said there would be a chance of light rain, but something of half a millimeter in total. My son-in-law was extremely optimistic, which I thought... was optimistic. 

The next morning, on the day of the event, my son-in-law, his dad, and I met early at the site to set up. The weather was a bit muggy but there wasn't a cloud in the sky. And it remained relatively like that through the ceremony and lunch (supplied by a food truck that served lobster rolls and other familiar foods that delighted the guests immensely). It was warm. It was quite warm. But far from the punishing heat of the early summer a month before. Everyone remarked on how lucky we had been with the weather. 

All that was before the clouds rolled in. We saw them to the north at first, as they came up on us quickly. And soon, in the mid-afternoon, people gathered back under the tent, and moved the Cornhole game under the large gazebo.
